Reading the proposal · The chapter text below is from Luke Nathan Hayes’s 27 October 2026 introduction. It describes a programme in development. Research, clinical and funding pathways retain their stated conditions. Dated checks and original sources are available alongside the unchanged document.

Adult Genesis and the Truth Engine research proposal

Aura Genesis is the proposed intensive adult programme bringing together self reflection, development of a personal Aura, hyperbaric oxygen therapy (HBOT) and the Truth Engine research proposal. It is designed to develop a richer human and twin partnership from the initial minimum viable self. Everyday Aura support has its own introduction pathway. [P4]

The proposed session experience

The source design describes 60 days with two hours per day at hyperbaric pressure, totalling 120 session hours. HBOT involves breathing oxygen in a pressurised chamber. A hyperbaric specialist assesses the person and the clinical purpose, with the actual schedule determined through the clinical and research arrangements. [P4, R34]

Sessions are intended as solo personal reflection, supported by the agreed interface and clinical team. An ordinary screen or virtual reality, a headset displaying an immersive environment, would depend on preference and verified equipment suitability. Discovery, personalisation and integration describe the proposed sequence: record the first account, connect its personal contexts, then revisit important claims and test how faithfully the twin represents the person. [P4]

Continuous recording during sessions

The Genesis design includes continuous camera and microphone recording throughout the defined sessions, including periods of rest. Specified sensors follow their agreed acquisition arrangements. Recording outside those session boundaries follows separate interaction permissions. The person receives an understandable demonstration of what is recorded, how a correction is made and how later use or sharing is agreed. [P4]

Clinical supervision, safe exit and emergency procedures take priority. The participant understands these arrangements before a first pressure session. The defined recording conditions are part of choosing Genesis participation; ordinary support offers a separate route for someone who does not want that experience.

What the Truth Engine is investigating

The research includes tasks in which an adult explicitly agrees to give selected truthful or deliberately false responses. The record identifies which response was requested for that task. Researchers would investigate whether personal speech, behaviour and other recorded signals provide useful information for the developing twin. Agreement to this research is explained separately from other programme elements. [P4]

This is research, rather than a validated lie detection service. There is no established basis for using its outputs to judge honesty, capacity or entitlement to care. Uncertain memory, communication differences and distress receive appropriate clinical consideration. Any future claim about performance requires its own evidence for the particular task and people involved.

The personal experience, the HBOT intervention and the Truth Engine hypotheses each need defined research questions. Their inclusion in a shared design does not establish a combined clinical benefit.

HBOT and other adult wellbeing activities

The adult proposal brings clinical treatment and wellbeing activities into the same wider setting while giving each a defined purpose. An activity is considered in relation to the person's health, interests, medication, access needs and comfort. Its place in a programme follows that assessment. [P9]

Healing goals and the evidence for HBOT

HBOT has recognised medical uses, including selected difficult wounds and radiation injuries. Published patient information from Royal Brisbane and Women's Hospital explains specialist assessment, treatment planning and repeated checks of fitness for treatment. It provides a reference for understanding clinical delivery and relevant healing goals. The information helps identify the assessment, supervision and treatment planning an actual offer would need. [R34]

Dementia use and the proposed combined Aura programme require their own evidence. A small 2020 study examined HBOT, including changes in thinking and memory test scores for people with Alzheimer's disease. Early improvements were not sustained at three and six months. This gives a question for controlled investigation, rather than evidence of lasting benefit from the proposed programme. [R35]

Chamber engineering, oxygen safety, permitted equipment, clinical monitoring and emergency arrangements belong to qualified hyperbaric delivery. Any interface or sensor used there requires verified suitability. Personal AI and presentation systems remain separate from the validated treatment controls. [P4, R34]

Sauna and ice baths in context

Sauna is among the proposed adult wellbeing options. A Finnish study followed a group of men and found more frequent sauna use was associated with a lower later risk of dementia. Participants were observed, rather than assigned sauna treatment, so the finding does not establish cause or treatment of existing dementia. Individual health and the purpose of participation need review. [R36]

Ice baths and other cold immersion also require a distinct assessment. Cold shock affects breathing, heart rate and blood pressure, making personal health and supervision relevant. Comfort, a clear way to stop and an appropriate response to difficulty are part of considering participation. [R37]

Wellbeing in everyday life

Movement, suitable recreation, food, sleep, rest and social contact remain part of the wider life being supported. A person might enjoy a garden activity, music, an accessible outing or a quiet period of reflection. Relevant practitioners help define any rehabilitation or clinical goals.

The programme records what was offered, why it was chosen and how the person experienced it. A pleasurable activity has value as an experience; a medical claim requires clinical evidence. Reviewing both helps the proposed setting develop around real people and useful outcomes.

A useful next step

Begin with something that matters

An actual research invitation needs to explain the activity, recording, clinical purpose, responsible team, alternatives and a practical way to stop. Everyday Aura support has its own introduction route.
