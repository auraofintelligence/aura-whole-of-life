The planned clinical and SaMD pathway

Aura's dementia research plan includes a pathway for Software as a Medical Device, abbreviated SaMD. This is software intended to perform a medical purpose, such as supporting clinical monitoring or treatment decisions. The proposed clinical functions connect personal narratives and everyday observations with professional review, then test whether defined outputs offer reliable clinical value. [P3]

Define the function and the intended people

The Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) regulates medical products in Australia. For software, its intended purpose means the medical job it is designed or claimed to perform. That purpose, its users and risks determine the applicable regulatory category and whether an exclusion or exemption applies. Organising general information and interpreting symptoms for treatment decisions raise different requirements. [R27, R30]

An understandable description identifies the particular input, output and decision being supported. For example, organising observations for review is a different intended function from recommending a treatment. That distinction makes the evidence question clearer and tells a clinician what responsibility the software is being offered to support.

Quality management made practical

Quality management means documented processes for designing, checking, changing and maintaining a dependable product. The planned work records each function, tests it against its design and assesses suitability for intended use. It also examines accessibility, ease of use, protection from unauthorised access and clinical evidence. Foreseeable failures and changes are reviewed against the purpose and risks of the version in use. [P3, R27]

An incorrect association between a person and a record, a missing observation or an unclear instruction has practical consequences. Testing therefore includes how an error is detected, how it reaches a responsible person and how the service continues when a function is unavailable. The person and worker also need an understandable account of uncertainty.

Clinical evidence and regulated supply

AI medical software needs evidence that is sufficiently transparent for safety and performance to be evaluated. Clinical validation concerns its intended purpose and population. The development plan therefore tests relevant performance, clinical usefulness, errors and bias, alongside the experience of using the interface. [R30]

For regulated supply, developers need evidence against the Essential Principles, the safety and performance requirements for medical devices. Applicable conformity assessment checks how those requirements are met. Inclusion in the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods, the national register for regulated products, is also needed unless an exemption applies. Safety monitoring, incident reporting and review of software changes continue after release. [R27]

The material reviewed describes this clinical development route. It does not report completed clinical trials or regulatory approval for the proposed medical functions. The present public prototype and the planned clinical product each receive a clear account of their actual functions and stage of development.

The staged research strategy and measures of benefit

The strategy begins with a defined use, then asks progressively stronger questions about safety, practicality and benefit. A study of an accessible personal record has a different question from a study of HBOT or a Truth Engine research task. Each clinical claim is linked to evidence designed to assess it. [P3-P4]

First establish feasibility

The existing dementia research plan proposes 10 to 15 person and carer pairs using the system at home for four to six weeks. The first questions concern acceptability, usability, burden and practical safety. Participant criteria, the final protocol and recruitment follow clinical and ethics review. [P3]

Feasibility means whether the proposed activity is practical and acceptable. This first study would examine whether onboarding produces a recognisable starting self, whether people find and correct material, and whether an activity fits daily life. It records help required and reasons for stopping. Those findings guide the next design decision rather than establishing every possible Aura benefit.

Refine the pilot and test effectiveness

A subsequent pilot, an initial study at one care or research location, would refine recruitment, assessment and delivery while estimating potential effects. A larger study across several locations would test effectiveness, costs and longer term outcomes against an appropriate comparison, such as usual care. Results and approvals at each stage determine the next step. [P3]

Research involving unapproved therapeutic goods uses the applicable TGA Clinical Trial Notification or Clinical Trial Approval pathway. These provide legal routes for supplying those goods in a trial. Notification does not mean TGA evaluation of the study data or approval of its clinical claims. Ethics review, site permission and the sponsor's duties remain separate requirements. [R31]

Match outcomes to the purpose

Personal goals are reviewed alongside clinical assessment tools studied for suitability and accuracy for their intended use. Outcomes might concern daily activities, quality of life, expression of preferences or a move. The effects on carers' workload and wellbeing, unwanted experiences, staff time and differences between participants also matter. Each measure answers a defined question. [P3]

A memory palace study might ask whether a resident finds a visit meaningful, whether conversation changes and whether the activity causes distress. A clinical software study tests the reliability and usefulness of its defined output. HBOT and other interventions require their own questions and outcome measures.

An agency also needs an account of delivery costs and actual resource use. The evaluation records what took place, who supplied support and where the activity added work. Findings then support a precise description of benefit and a justified decision about extending the service.

A useful next step

Begin with something that matters

Describe one input, one output and the decision being supported. A clear purpose makes it possible to choose useful outcomes, examine errors and decide what the next study should test.
