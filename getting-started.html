Recording a minimum viable self during onboarding

The first purpose of onboarding is to record enough of the person for the intended human and twin partnership to begin usefully. Minimum refers to the starting information needed for the agreed functions. Viable means that those uses have a recognisable personal foundation, with missing information and uncertainty visible. The record then develops through experience. [P4, pages 4 to 5]

If the person proceeds, they and a facilitator, someone helping with the introduction, identify the first useful functions. A care handover profile might connect identity, communication preferences, trusted relationships, current circumstances, routines and meaningful memories. They review a proposed introduction and the records informing it. Accessible conversation, selected existing records and chosen media help assemble this first version. The facilitator's role and qualifications are explained for the activity involved.

What a starting self record brings together
Part of the starting selfWhat the first record brings together
Identity and body contextChosen identity, relevant body information, current circumstances and the sensory, movement or communication needs that affect the intended use.
Language and meaningThe person's own words, preferred explanations, values, beliefs, important interests and ways of expressing agreement or discomfort.
People and placesImportant relationships, who offers support, meaningful homes and locations, and the connections the person wants remembered.
Life history and present needsSelected memories and events, existing skills, routines, preferences and relevant health or support information with its source.
Intentions and boundariesWhat the person wants help with, future wishes, intended twin functions and the agreed audiences and uses of the information.

A first version the person recognises

A facilitator returns the proposed record in an understandable form. The person changes wording, corrects an interpretation and identifies what remains unanswered. A family contribution retains its authorship. A clinical document remains distinguishable from a personal recollection. Information that has not been reviewed stays visibly unchecked.

The starting profile is reviewed against the agreed functions. A field holds an answer or an explicit unresolved status. Missing information changes what is attempted next. The result is a usable first account with a review date, rather than an assumption that the whole person has been captured. [P4, page 8]

Developing the human and twin partnership

The minimum viable self gives later encounters somewhere to begin. In Aura's proposed human and twin relationship, records develop through reflection, practical activity and correction. The person supplies meaning as well as information. An account of what happened becomes more useful when the person also explains its significance. [P4, P7]

Connections that make information useful

A photograph of a kitchen is more useful when linked to the person who lived there, a favourite recipe and the reason a particular routine mattered. A communication preference is more useful when its circumstances are understood. A new worker learning that someone likes a quiet introduction receives a practical starting point for their first meeting.

Aura's visual design draws on aura and chakra imagery, using connected visual forms to organise personal information. Everyday use is intended to suit the task: conversation, an ordinary screen or an optional three dimensional view explored through a screen or headset. These are ways to navigate records; their usefulness is tested through the person's experience. [P7-P8]

A personal model that learns with its person

The longer design includes a compact personal language model, sometimes called a nanoLLM. A language model is software that learns patterns from examples to generate language; Aura proposes authorised personal examples, reflections and corrections for that learning. Its tasks and information boundaries would be agreed before training. Testing would examine faithful representation, uncertainty and correction. This remains planned development beyond the present browser prototype. [P4, page 27; P8]

Keeping a record, retrieving a passage, producing an interpretation and changing a trained model are different activities. The person receives an explanation of what a particular encounter does. A correction to a story is retained as a correction, with a clear account of which records or model versions have incorporated it.

Continuity with room for change

A person's present wishes sit alongside earlier experience. A favourite song may become irritating; a crowded gathering may give way to a quieter visit. Dates and context help the system retain that history while support responds to the person now. New friendships, creative work and ambitions join the record.

The intended value is cumulative. Something meaningful recorded during onboarding helps a later encounter, and that encounter improves the next version of the personal account. An ongoing relationship develops around the life being lived, rather than treating the first profile as a final description.

A useful next step

Begin with something that matters

What would you like the first useful encounter to help you do? A short care introduction, a meaningful activity or a question for someone supporting you can provide a beginning.
