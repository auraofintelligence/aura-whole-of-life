NDIS aged care and funding transitions

The person's life continues across funding boundaries. Aura is intended to retain useful context when support changes, while the actual programme determines eligibility, approved services and payment. Clinical care, disability supports, aged care, research and privately funded activities have different arrangements.

The disability support pathway

The National Disability Insurance Scheme, or NDIS, funds eligible disability supports under its rules. An applicant needs to be under 65 when applying. Existing participants have the choice to remain in the scheme after 65; permanent entry to residential aged care after 65 requires leaving it. Other eligibility requirements also apply. [R17, R22]

A proposed Aura support needs to address the relevant disability related need and fit the funding rules. Assistive technology decisions consider evidence, risk, value and the responsibilities of other service systems. A diagnosis or interest in a technology does not by itself establish funding for a particular item. [R16]

The aged care pathway

My Aged Care is the government entry point for aged care assessment and services. Assessment access generally begins at 65, with specified pathways from 50 for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people and people who are homeless or at risk of homelessness. Needs and circumstances also matter. [R18]

Support at Home, introduced in November 2025, funds assessed aged care services to help people live at home. Its Assistive Technology and Home Modifications scheme provides separate assessed funding for eligible equipment and changes to the home. Relevant practitioners may be involved. An Aura proposal needs to fit the actual programme rules, assessment and support being requested. [R19-R20]

A transparent arrangement before participation

The proposed activity identifies who is paying, what is included and what further costs might arise. Research funding and demonstration costs are identified separately from the person's ongoing care budget. Medical device regulation, provider registration and individual funding approval are separate processes.

A clinical intervention such as HBOT has its own clinical and payment arrangements. The proposal does not represent automatic NDIS or aged care funding for Aura or its intensive adult programme. The responsible organisation confirms a concrete offer against the relevant rules before asking the person to agree.

When services change, the person and relevant professionals settle what information follows, who receives access and which responsibilities transfer. The personal life record offers continuity, while the actual funding and care arrangements remain clear to everyone involved.

Population context and a local Queensland beginning

The need spans adults with disability, families adapting to dementia and older people whose support is increasing. Population data from the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) helps describe the setting for planning. Individual circumstances and assessment determine who a particular support fits.

Dementia and disability across adult life

The Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (AIHW) estimates about 439,000 Australians lived with dementia in 2025 and projects about 459,000 in 2026. Its younger onset estimate is about 29,500 people aged 30 to 64 in 2025. Exact prevalence is uncertain. The ABS 2022 disability survey estimated 5.5 million Australians, or 21.4%, had disability, a broader population than NDIS participants. [R4-R5, R6]

Begin with a defined local need

Brisbane and the surrounding region offer a setting for cooperation between home based support, disability services, aged care and clinical research. A focused beginning might examine continuity through a change of home, including the starting self record and selected memory palace activities. Results inform whether and how to extend the work.

A project proposal concerning 82 Claytons Road at Amity Point on Minjerribah, also known as North Stradbroke Island, explores a place for memory palaces, whole person support and community participation. It is a property and partnership proposal. A care or research service there would depend on agreements, suitable premises, qualified delivery and approvals. [P1]

Local development also needs practical access arrangements for people and supporters. Transport, communication, equipment, staff time and cultural participation form part of a concrete delivery plan. First Nations participation and cultural permissions are developed with the people and communities concerned.

Royal Commission lessons and international good practice

A Royal Commission is a formal public inquiry that investigates issues and recommends reform. Australia's Aged Care Quality and Safety inquiry reported in 2021; its Disability Royal Commission reported in 2023. Their findings explain problems a new care proposal needs to address. They provide criteria for assessing whether proposed care addresses those problems. [R8-R9]

Apply the lessons in the actual encounter

The aged care inquiry identified failures in dementia care, nutrition and restrictive practices, which limit a person's movement or freedom. A personal record helps only when staff have time, skills and responsibility to respond. Aura proposes relevant context at the point of care, a clear response to concerns and evaluation of the resulting experience. [R8]

The Disability Royal Commission's work on autonomy and access reinforces accessible communication, supported decisions and independent advocacy. A personal record helps when the person is able to express and revise preferences, identify where an account came from and reach someone responsible for a decision. [R9]

The Aged Care Act 2024, governing Australian Government funded aged care, commenced on 1 November 2025 alongside strengthened quality standards. The law and standards address rights, provider duties, individual choice, dignity and appropriate care. A proposed Aura service needs to identify its actual role and responsibilities within this setting. [R10, R12]

Use international guidance for a defined purpose

WHO's 2025 Integrated Care for Older People guidance links capacities, needs and priorities with coordinated health and social support. That informs the intended connection between a personal goal, the people assisting and the review of what helped. [R13]

The UK's National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) publishes clinical guidelines. Its dementia guidance recommends tailored activities and consideration of reminiscence, cognitive rehabilitation or occupational therapy for relevant needs. Reminiscence uses past experiences in conversation; occupational therapy helps adapt meaningful daily activities and surroundings. These approaches help frame activity design and the professional input needed. [R14]

Alzheimer's Disease International, a federation of dementia associations, publishes the World Alzheimer Report. Its 2020 Design Dignity Dementia edition examines supportive physical environments across cultures. This matters because a digital collection sits within a real home: familiar objects, understandable surroundings and relationships continue to shape daily experience. [R15]

A precise contribution to better care

The evaluation asks whether Aura adds something the person values while fitting the realities of support. Findings distinguish a helpful conversation, a specific functional benefit and a clinical outcome. Claims about disease progression, healing, staffing savings or wider system effects require evidence designed to test those particular claims.

A useful next step

Begin with something that matters

Set out who is paying, what is included, what support is needed and who is accountable. The Amity land proposal and a funded care or research service each require their own agreements.
