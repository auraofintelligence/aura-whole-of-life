  2. 01

    Getting started

    Choose one useful purpose, record enough personal context to begin, and review the result in your own words. The relationship develops through experience and correction.

    ↗
  3. 02

    Changing support needs

    Younger onset dementia, disability and emerging aged care involve different lives and support arrangements. Begin with the person’s communication, abilities and chosen goals.

    ↗
  4. 03

    Identity and future wishes

    Explore why an experience or preference matters. Earlier stories sit alongside present wishes, new friendships and possibilities still to come.

    ↗
  5. 04

    Memory palaces and downsizing

    A familiar room, garden or object can lead back to a story. A place-based collection preserves selected photographs, recordings and their personal meaning through a move.

    ↗
  6. 05

    Moving home and into care

    Prepare a small, useful introduction for the receiving team. Earlier places remain part of a life that continues in a new room, with present routines and relationships.

    ↗
  7. 06

    Adult Genesis and wellbeing

    The intensive adult Genesis design brings personal reflection, HBOT and Truth Engine research into a proposed programme. Each element needs its own purpose, assessment and evidence.

    ↗
  8. 07

    People, consent and safety

    Families, carers, providers, clinicians and researchers bring different responsibilities. A dependable offer makes those responsibilities and the person’s choices understandable.

    ↗
  9. 08

    Personal records and legal context

    Understand where information is held, who can use it and how to find or correct it. Legal learning helps prepare a clearer question and identify the responsible decision-maker.

    ↗
  10. 09

    Clinical development and evidence

    A personal record, a clinical software function, HBOT and a Truth Engine task ask different questions. The proposed development route connects each claim with a suitable evaluation.

    ↗
  11. 10

    Care pathways and a local beginning

    Funding pathways, quality standards and local relationships shape what can actually be offered. A focused Queensland beginning would examine a defined need and the resources around it.

    ↗
  12. 11

    Community, work and time

    Friendship, learning, creativity, rest and contribution belong alongside support needs. Wider Aura proposals explore voluntary reciprocity, shared enterprise and time throughout life.

    ↗
  13. 12

    Developing the programme

    Prepare a demonstration, name the people and resources involved, and establish readiness before recruitment. The next step follows a review of the actual experience.
